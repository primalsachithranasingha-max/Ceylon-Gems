The Ratna Dweepa Legacy

For over twenty-five centuries, Sri Lanka has been celebrated in ancient Sanskrit scriptures as *Ratna Dweepa* (The Island of Gems). Historic chroniclers from King Solomon's court to Marco Polo recorded that Ceylon possessed the most radiant sapphires, rubies, and chrysoberyls on Earth.

Royalty & Historic Lineage

Gemological Buyer's Guide

Understanding Colored Gemstones

Unlike diamonds, colored gems are evaluated by the harmony of hue, tone, saturation, and natural crystallization silk.

C1

Color & Saturation

The preeminent factor in Ceylon sapphires. We look for vivid 'Cornflower' and 'Royal' blues without dark undertones, or the delicate lotus balance in Padparadscha.

C2

Clarity & Natural Silk

Fine microscopic rutile needles ('silk') prove a stone is natural and unheated, giving Ceylon sapphires their velvety royal glow.

C3

Cut & Proportion

Our master lapidaries facet each rough gemstone to retain maximum natural weight while aligning optical axes for scintillation and internal fire.

C4

Carat Mass

High-clarity unheated Ceylon sapphires exceeding 3 to 5 carats are exponential in rarity and serve as timeless portfolio-grade assets.
